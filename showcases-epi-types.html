Showcases · Labelling · ePI types

FHIR ePI Types 1–4, shown on one example label

The HL7 ePI implementation guide lets companies and regulators move from documents to data in four steps. Type 1 publishes the approved SmPC and package leaflet as FHIR documents with coded headings. Type 2 adds the product: packs, ingredients and authorisations. Type 3 adds the clinical particulars as coded data. Type 4 links the words of the approved text to that data, so the label can be checked, filtered and personalised. The eleven showcases below are grouped by audience. Types 1, 3 and 4 take one fictitious product, Sakura Bloom 20 mg film-coated tablets, through the SmPC, package leaflet and instructions for use. Type 2 uses the WonderDrug powder for oral suspension from the HL7 ePI implementation guide to show the difference between the manufactured item and the administrable product.

The four types

Each type contains everything in the type before it · open the ePI viewer →
Type 1

Digital label

The approved SmPC and leaflet as FHIR documents: coded headings, XHTML narrative and images as Binary.

 Type 2

Product identification

Adds the product, packs with GTINs, manufactured item, administrable product, ingredients, substances, authorisations and organisations, referenced from the sections.

 Type 3

Clinical information

Adds the indication with its dosing, contraindications, warnings, interactions and undesirable effects as coded ClinicalUseDefinitions.

 Type 4

Dynamic digital label

Links each phrase of the approved text to the resource that carries its meaning, with textLink, so the label can be personalised and kept in step with the data.

About the data

Synthetic example data only. Sakura Bloom and its active substance Sakuramil are fictitious; the product comes from the ICH M4Q(R2) Sakura Bloom mock-up, which describes its quality data but not its clinical use. Every sentence of the SmPC and of both leaflets was written for this showcase, and the indication, dosing, contraindications, interactions, pharmacology and undesirable effects are invented. No text is taken from a real product label. Do not use any of it for a clinical purpose.

The product, tablet, ingredient, substance and site resources are reused from the Sakura Bloom PQI dataset on this site. Pack sizes, GTINs (GS1 restricted-circulation prefix 02), marketing authorisation numbers and the marketing authorisation holder are invented. The document structure follows the HL7 ePI IG and its WonderDrug examples; SmPC headings use the EMA SPOR codes, while leaflet headings and a few concepts use local example codes because verified codes were not available. Every resource is tagged HTEST. The bundles have not been run through the HL7 FHIR validator.
